## One-Pager

### Problem

Your board, council, or commissioners just asked a simple question after a peer ransomware story: *“Are we okay?”* Your IT lead can keep systems running — but there is no current risk assessment, no board-ready summary, and no clear map to NIST CSF 2.0 or state expectations. Grant applications ask for a cybersecurity plan. Insurance or municipal pools want evidence of basic controls. Water and other critical departments add operational risk you cannot ignore.

You do not have budget for a full-time CISO. You do need a defensible starting picture — grant-eligible language, peer-benchmarked priorities, and a briefing elected officials can stand behind.

### Solution

Hudson Valley CISO’s **Cybersecurity Assessment** is a fixed-scope discovery engagement for municipalities, counties, districts, and public utilities. We produce a practical baseline: what you have, what matters most, and what to do next — without requiring new hires.

**What you get:**
- Stakeholder discovery with IT, leadership, and (as needed) operations / water / public safety
- Lightweight asset and critical-service view (what must stay up for the public)
- Risk and control snapshot aligned to **NIST CSF 2.0** (and CISA Cross-Sector CPGs where helpful)
- Prioritized roadmap (30 / 60 / 90 days and longer-horizon items)
- **Board / council briefing** — plain language, taxpayer-defensible
- Grant-ready language you can reuse in DHS / FEMA / state infrastructure applications (where applicable)

**Timeline:** Typically 2–4 weeks from kickoff to briefing, depending on scheduling and document access.

### Proof

This assessment is **framework-first** (NIST CSF 2.0), vendor-neutral, and designed for SLED realities: limited staff, procurement constraints, and public accountability. We do not invent past performance or fake peer metrics. Where peers are referenced, we speak in patterns (“jurisdictions your size usually…”) unless you authorize a named reference.

### Next Step

Need a board-ready starting picture for your municipality, district, or utility? Visit [hudsonvalleyciso.com](https://hudsonvalleyciso.com) to schedule a discovery call. Soft follow-up: free assessment entry point also available via [securitymedic.com](https://securitymedic.com).

## Talk Track

### Opening (30 seconds)

“Most local governments I meet don’t need a Hollywood threat briefing. They need a calm answer for the board: here’s what we have, here’s what would hurt the public first, and here’s a fixed-scope plan that fits your staff and budget. That’s what this assessment is — a NIST CSF–aligned snapshot and a briefing you can take to council without jargon.”

### Discovery Questions (3–5 key questions)

1. **“What triggered this conversation — peer incident, insurance/pool question, grant deadline, or a board ask?”**
2. **“Who owns cyber risk today — IT alone, or is there a named executive / board liaison?”**
3. **“Which services must stay up for residents — finance, 911/dispatch, water SCADA/OT adjacency, permitting, schools?”**
4. **“Do you have a current written risk assessment, IR plan, and backup restore test evidence?”**
5. **“Are you preparing any grant applications that ask for a cybersecurity plan or risk assessment?”**

### Pitch (2–3 minutes)

“Here’s how we run it. We interview the right people, review what documentation you already have, and map gaps to NIST CSF 2.0 in language elected officials understand. You leave with three things: a prioritized risk picture, a practical roadmap your IT team can execute with or without us, and a one-page board briefing.

We work *with* your IT lead — we don’t replace them. They keep operations; we add the governance and documentation layer boards and grant reviewers expect. Fixed scope, fixed price. No fake SOC claims. No product pitch.”

### Objection Handling

- **“We don’t have budget.”** → “Understood. Many jurisdictions fund discovery through grants or phase work. We can structure the deliverable so the assessment itself strengthens a grant application. We’ll also price it as a fraction of one civil-service cybersecurity hire.”
- **“Our IT person can handle this.”** → “They should stay in the driver’s seat. We provide the CISO-style assessment and board packaging most IT teams weren’t hired to produce — then hand the roadmap back to them.”
- **“We need board approval first.”** → “We’ll give you a one-page cost justification and peer-framed rationale you can put on the next agenda. Happy to attend if that helps.”
- **“We’re a water / utility operator — OT is different.”** → “We treat water and other critical services as first-class in scoping: what can interrupt service, who vendors are, and what IT/OT boundaries exist. We stay honest about OT depth — assessment first, then specialized follow-on if needed.”
- **“What if we’re audited later?”** → “Deliverables are yours — risk summary, roadmap, briefing. They’re meant to stand without us. Audit support can be an add-on if you want it later.”

### Close

“Next step is a short discovery call to confirm trigger, decision-makers, and whether grant timing matters. I’ll send a fixed-scope proposal and a board-ready one-pager. Fair?”
