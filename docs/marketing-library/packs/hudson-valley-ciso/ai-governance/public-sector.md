### Regulatory & Framework Notes

- **NIST AI RMF 1.0:** Four functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Voluntary framework for AI risk management. Federal agencies are encouraged to adopt; state/local not required but increasingly expected by auditors and grant reviewers.
- **NIST AI 600-1 (Generative AI Profile):** Companion to AI RMF, specific to generative AI (LLMs, image gen, etc.). Covers transparency, data privacy, harmful bias, confabulation.
- **No federal AI compliance mandate for SLED yet (as of Sept 2026):** AI governance is proactive, not reactive to regulation. EU AI Act is in force in EU; no US equivalent yet. Some states considering AI regulation but none passed as of Sept 2026.
- **Public records laws (FOIL, state equivalents):** Vary by state. FOIL (NY Freedom of Information Law) requires disclosure of government records. AI usage may trigger records requests. Some jurisdictions are starting to treat AI-generated drafts as records; others distinguish between research/brainstorming (not records) and official communication (records).
- **Data residency and sovereignty:** Some jurisdictions have policies requiring government data to stay in-state or in-country. AI vendors often use multi-region cloud infrastructure. Contract review is critical.
- **Law enforcement data:** CJIS (Criminal Justice Information Services) standards apply to law enforcement systems. AI tools processing CJIS data must meet additional security requirements. FBI CJIS Security Policy is the standard.

### Public Sector Unique Considerations

- **Civil service hiring limits:** Most jurisdictions can't compete for cybersecurity talent on salary. Fractional CISO is often the only viable model.
- **Procurement and RFPs:** Competitive bidding, lowest responsible bidder, multi-signature approval. Longer sales cycles than private sector.
- **Public accountability:** Decisions must be defensible to taxpayers, media, and elected officials. "Peer benchmarking" and "grant-eligible" are compelling arguments.
- **Transparency expectations:** Constituents expect government to disclose AI usage. Proactive transparency (publish AI policy, approved tool list) builds trust.
- **Constituent data sensitivity:** Varies by department — law enforcement (CJIS), health/social services (HIPAA/Medicaid), tax records (IRS 1075), court records. AI governance must account for mixed data types.
- **Multi-department complexity:** AI usage varies widely by department. Finance uses AI for fraud detection, HR uses AI for resume screening, clerks use AI for public records search. One-size-fits-all policy won't work; must be flexible by department while maintaining consistent risk standards.
