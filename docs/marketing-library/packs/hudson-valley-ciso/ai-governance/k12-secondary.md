### Objection Handling

#### "We banned ChatGPT. Problem solved."

**Response:**  
"I understand the instinct, but here's the reality: students can access ChatGPT on their phones, at home, or on personal devices. Teachers are using it on personal accounts for lesson planning. A ban drives AI usage underground where you have zero visibility. More importantly, AI is embedded in tools you already use — Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Clever, even some library databases now have AI features. A blanket ban doesn't address that. A better approach: approve safe AI tools with clear guardrails, prohibit high-risk tools, and educate students and staff on responsible AI use. That's enforceable and defensible."

#### "Our IT team doesn't have time for this."

**Response:**  
"Exactly why we exist. Your IT team is managing 5,000 devices, network uptime, help desk tickets, and a dozen other priorities. #### "What about parental consent? Don't we need permission before students use AI?"

**Response:**  
"Great question. Under FERPA, districts can share student data with 'school officials' — including EdTech vendors — without parental consent IF there's a written Data Processing Agreement and the tool serves a legitimate educational purpose. That's the DPA we help you establish. For AI tools that go beyond educational use — like marketing or research — yes, you'd need parental consent. We'll flag those for you and help you determine when consent is required. Most classroom AI tools fall under the school official exception if the contract is structured correctly."

#### "Teachers will complain if we restrict tools. AI is useful for differentiation and personalized learning."

**Response:**  
"Absolutely, and we're not proposing you ban all AI. The goal is to give teachers a vetted list of AI tools that are FERPA-compliant and safe for student data. Teachers can use those freely. For new tools, we set up a fast-track review process so teachers aren't waiting months for approval. It's about making FERPA compliance invisible to teachers while still protecting student data. We've seen peer districts successfully balance both."

#### "What if parents panic when they hear we're using AI with students?"

**Response:**  
"That's why the parent FAQ is part of the deliverable. It's proactive, transparent communication: 'Here's how our district governs AI. Here's the list of approved tools. Here's how we protect student data. Here's how parents can opt out if they choose.' Transparency builds trust. The districts that get in trouble are the ones parents find out about AI usage through media or rumor. You're getting ahead of that."

### Regulatory & Framework Notes

- **FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act):** Protects student education records. Applies to all schools receiving federal funding. Requires written agreements (DPAs) for third-party service providers. 34 CFR § 99.31(a)(1) is the "school official" exception.
- **COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act):** Applies to online services collecting personal information from children under 13. Schools can consent on behalf of parents for educational purposes. Overlaps with FERPA but distinct law.
- **NIST AI RMF 1.0:** Four functions — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. Voluntary framework. No federal mandate for K-12 AI governance yet, but NIST AI RMF is becoming the de facto standard.
- **State student data privacy laws:** Many states (including NY) have student data privacy laws that layer on top of FERPA. Check state-specific requirements. NY Ed Law § 2-d covers third-party vendor data security and privacy.
- **No AI compliance mandate for K-12 yet (as of Sept 2026):** AI governance is proactive. Some states are considering AI education bills but none passed yet. EU AI Act has provisions for education but doesn't apply to US districts.

### K-12 Unique Considerations

- **Teacher buy-in is critical:** If teachers see AI governance as "IT blocking useful tools," it will fail. Frame as "protecting students while enabling innovation."
- **Parent communication:** Transparency is essential. Proactive FAQs and opt-out processes build trust. Reactive ("we didn't tell parents, now they're panicking") is a crisis.
- **Student age matters:** Elementary (COPPA applies, under 13), middle/high school (FERPA applies, COPPA doesn't). Adjust language and parental consent processes accordingly.
- **Chromebook/Google Workspace prevalence:** Most K-12 districts are Google-heavy. Google Workspace for Education has AI features (Duet AI, etc.). Must be reviewed for FERPA compliance.
- **EdTech vendor landscape:** Many EdTech vendors added AI features post-contract. Districts may have signed agreements before AI was in the product. Requires contract amendment or DPA update.
