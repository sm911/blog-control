# Audience Profile: SMB (Small & Medium Business)

**Last Updated:** September 2026

---

## Overview

Small and medium businesses (SMB) are Security Medic's core audience. These organizations typically have:
- 10–500 employees
- Limited or no dedicated security staff
- An IT manager or outsourced MSP handling day-to-day technology
- Revenue under $100M
- Regulated or compliance-driven operations (healthcare, finance, professional services) OR
- B2B relationships requiring vendor security questionnaires

SMBs are reactive buyers. They seek security help when an external event forces action, not proactively.

---

## Pain Points

### 1. **The Compliance Questionnaire They Can't Answer**
A large customer, insurance carrier, or regulatory body sends a security questionnaire. The IT manager looks at questions like "Do you have a formal vulnerability management program?" or "Is your CISO available for a call?" and realizes they have no answers.

### 2. **Cyber Insurance Renewal Crisis**
The insurance carrier raises premiums 300% or denies renewal entirely due to "inadequate security controls." The CFO is furious. The broker says "you need MFA, backups, and a security policy" but the org doesn't know where to start.

### 3. **First-Time Audit or Compliance Mandate**
HIPAA audit notice. SOC 2 requirement from a new enterprise customer. NYDFS 23 NYCRR 500 annual certification deadline. The org has never been through this before and doesn't know what "compliance" means in practice.

### 4. **Board or Investor Due Diligence Failure**
Potential investor or acquirer runs security due diligence. Findings report comes back: "No incident response plan. No vendor risk program. Passwords stored in shared Excel file." Deal stalls. Board asks "why didn't we know about this?"

### 5. **Shadow IT / Shadow AI Sprawl**
Employees are using ChatGPT, Grammarly, personal Dropbox, unapproved SaaS tools. IT has no visibility. Privacy officer (if one exists) is panicking about PHI or PII exposure. No one knows how to inventory or govern this.

## Buying Triggers

SMBs buy security services when an external deadline or consequence forces action:

| Trigger | Urgency | Decision Maker |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| Insurance renewal denied or premium spike | 30–60 days | CFO, broker |
| Customer compliance requirement for contract renewal | 60–90 days | VP Sales, IT Manager |
| Regulatory audit notice (HIPAA, NYDFS, etc.) | 90–180 days | CEO, Compliance Officer |
| M&A due diligence failure | Immediate–30 days | CEO, CFO, Board |
| Peer breach (competitor, industry peer) | 30–90 days | CEO, IT Manager |
| MSP says "we can't help with this" | Immediate | IT Manager, CFO |

**Key insight:** SMBs do not have budget allocated for security. Budget appears when a trigger event occurs. Pricing must be tied to avoiding a specific loss (insurance increase, lost contract, regulatory fine).

---

## Language: What Resonates

### Use:
- **"Pass your insurance questionnaire"** — Concrete, immediate outcome.
- **"Meet [specific regulation] requirements"** — Clarity. They want a checklist.
- **"Avoid the $X fine / lost contract / premium increase"** — Cost avoidance, not abstract risk.
- **"Your IT team stays focused on operations; we handle compliance"** — Reassurance. They're already overwhelmed.
- **"Framework-first: NIST CSF 2.0, HIPAA Security Rule"** — Legitimacy. They want to do this "the right way."
- **"Fixed-price package"** — Predictable cost. SMBs cannot budget for hourly consulting.

### Avoid:
- **"Advanced persistent threat"** — Too abstract. They don't care about nation-state actors.
- **"Zero trust architecture"** — Jargon. Sounds expensive and complicated.
- **"We'll build you a custom security program"** — Scary. Sounds like a year-long, six-figure project.
- **"You're at high risk of breach"** — Fear without actionable next step = paralysis.
- **Hourly rates or open-ended engagements** — Budget uncertainty kills deals.

## Proof That Lands

SMBs trust peers, brokers, and frameworks more than vendor claims. Effective proof includes:

### 1. **"Other [industry] orgs like yours have used this to [outcome]"**
- "We helped a 50-person medical practice pass their HIPAA audit and reduce insurance premiums by 40%."
- "A Hudson Valley manufacturer used our vCISO service to meet their Fortune 500 customer's security requirements and keep the contract."

(Only use real examples with permission. If no real example exists, say "This program is designed for orgs facing [trigger]" instead of inventing a case.)

### 2. **Framework Alignment**
- "This program aligns to NIST CSF 2.0 — the same framework your insurance carrier and auditors recognize."
- "We use the HIPAA Security Rule's required and addressable specifications as the roadmap."

Frameworks = legitimacy. SMBs want to follow an established standard, not a consultant's opinion.

### 3. **Insurance Broker Endorsement**
- "Your broker can review our deliverables before your renewal."
- "We've worked with [broker name if approved] on similar placements."

Brokers are trusted advisors for SMBs. Their endorsement carries weight.

### 4. **Fixed Deliverables List**
- "You'll get: 12-month security roadmap, incident response plan template, vendor risk questionnaire, quarterly board report."

SMBs want to see exactly what they're paying for. Vague "advisory services" descriptions do not close deals.

### 5. **Timeline & Effort**
- "Most SMBs complete this in 60–90 days with 2–4 hours per week of your team's time."

SMBs fear that "security" means hiring three new people or shutting down operations for a month. Realistic timeline + effort estimates build trust.

## Objection Handling

### Objection: "We already have an IT company / MSP."
**Response:**  
"That's great — we work alongside your IT team. They handle operations (servers, help desk, patching). We handle compliance, risk governance, and board reporting. Most MSPs don't have CISO-level expertise on staff, so we fill that gap. Your MSP stays in place; we just add the compliance layer."

### Objection: "We can't afford a CISO."
**Response:**  
"That's exactly why fractional CISO services exist. A full-time CISO costs $150K–$250K+ per year. Our Tier 1 fractional service is [price], and you get CISO-level expertise without the full-time overhead. You only pay for the hours you need."

### Objection: "We're not sure we need this yet."
**Response:**  
"Fair question. Most orgs come to us when something forces action — an insurance renewal, a customer compliance requirement, or an audit notice. If you don't have an external deadline right now, we recommend starting with a free security assessment. That'll tell you where your gaps are, and you can decide if and when to address them. No pressure."

### Objection: "Can't we just buy [vendor product] to fix this?"
**Response:**  
"Tools help, but compliance isn't just about buying software. HIPAA, NYDFS, SOC 2 — they all require policies, training, documentation, and governance. A tool might solve one piece (like MFA or backups), but it won't get you through an audit or pass a customer questionnaire. We help you build the full program, and we're vendor-neutral — we'll recommend tools that fit your budget and tech stack."

## Sales Cycle

SMB sales cycles are short when triggered, but stall without urgency:

| Phase | Duration | Key Activity |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| **Awareness** | 0–30 days | Trigger event → web search → contact form |
| **Discovery** | 1 meeting | Qualify: What's the deadline? What's the consequence? Who's the decision maker? |
| **Proposal** | 3–7 days | Send one-pager + pricing. SMBs decide quickly if budget exists. |
| **Close** | 1–2 meetings | SOW review, kickoff scheduling. SMBs don't negotiate much — they say yes or no. |

**Critical:** If there's no external deadline (insurance, audit, contract), the deal will stall indefinitely. Qualify for urgency in the first call.

---

## Ideal Service Fit

SMBs are best served by:
- **Fractional CISO — Tier 1 (Part-Time):** 8–16 hrs/month. Policy development, roadmap, quarterly check-ins.
- **Compliance Program Packages:** HIPAA, NYDFS, SOC 2. Fixed scope, deliverable-based.
- **Cyber Insurance Program Build:** Gap assessment, control remediation, questionnaire prep.
- **Shadow AI Sprint:** 2-week AI tool inventory + remediation roadmap.

SMBs do NOT buy:
- Open-ended advisory retainers (no defined outcome)
- Multi-year strategic programs (they need results in 60–90 days)
- Incident response retainers (unless they've been breached before)

---

## Key Takeaway

SMBs are outcome-driven buyers with limited budgets and urgent deadlines. Win their business by:
1. Speaking to the specific trigger (insurance, audit, contract).
2. Offering fixed-price, deliverable-based packages.
3. Using framework authority (NIST, HIPAA) instead of vendor FUD.
4. Showing them what "done" looks like with clear timelines and effort estimates.

No fake metrics. No invented case studies. If proof doesn't exist, say "This program is designed for orgs like yours facing [trigger]" and move on.
